RAC • EPI • APN — Full Guide

RAC establishes the hazard risk category. EPI is the normal DIMS execution-priority method. APN is the safety-hazard abatement priority method. This guide keeps those functions distinct while showing how they connect.

RACEPIAPNExamples

1. RAC — Risk Assessment Code

DAFI 91-202 describes RAC as an expression of the degree of risk associated with a hazard that combines hazard severity and mishap probability into one numeric identifier. For safety and fire hazards, Probability A–D is plotted against Severity I–IV.

Severity IDeath, permanent total disability, or loss of facility/asset of $2.5 million or more.
Severity IIPermanent partial disability or major property damage from $600,000 up to $2.5 million.
Severity IIILost-workday/compensable injury or property damage from $60,000 up to $600,000.
Severity IVFirst aid/minor supportive treatment, minimal threat, damage up to $60,000, or standards violation.
Probability ALikely to occur immediately.
Probability B–DB: probably in time. C: possible in time. D: unlikely to occur.

Governance: DAFI 91-202 states that only qualified occupational safety, fire, and/or health personnel shall assign a RAC to a hazard. DOME's interactive matrix is an assessment aid and does not silently replace an authoritative System RAC.

2. EPI — Execution Priority Index

EPI is a DIMS methodology, not an Air Force formula. It is the normal priority method for non-safety-hazard projects and tasks. Active DIMS measurement no longer uses the legacy human-time/AI-time pair. The current automated burden model uses Impact together with system-derived execution and participation signals.

Active measures: HEI + AEL + RWL + VOY + Autonomy
Final EPI weighting/calibration remains under validation; no unsupported numeric formula is asserted here.
FactorMeaningSource
ImpactMission, dependency, user, operational, or deliverable effectGoverned assessment

EPI governance: prioritize using Impact and the automated burden/outcome measures above. Exact weighting and score calibration remain governed under MPS-0001 and must be validated from observed sessions before a final numerical EPI formula is institutionalized.

3. APN — Abatement Priority Number

DAFI 91-202 defines APN as a two-part code: the RAC and the Cost Effectiveness Index (CEI). APN is used to determine relative priority of safety-hazard abatement actions.

CEI = C ÷ (M × E)
APN = RAC (CEI)
M — MultiplierSelected from the severity/probability multiplier matrix using the same Severity and Probability used for RAC.
E — ExposureAverage number of personnel exposed daily during the year.
C — CostTotal abatement project cost, using the best available estimate if actual cost is unknown.

DAFI 91-202 APN worked example: Severity II + Probability B gives RAC 2 and multiplier M = 21. If E = 25 people/day and C = $2,100, then CEI = 2,100 ÷ (21 × 25) = 4. Therefore APN = 2(4).
Priority rule: APNs are not used to compare projects of different RACs. Compare CEI only within identical RACs; lower CEI means higher relative abatement priority within that RAC.
